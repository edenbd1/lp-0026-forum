# Solution: LP-0026 — Logos Forum: a serverless forum where every author is verified

**Submitted by:** edenbd1

## Summary

Logos Forum is a Basecamp app for public, open discussion with no server
anywhere. Topics and replies travel over **Logos Delivery**; history is kept
on each device and shared through **Logos Storage**; every post is Ed25519-signed
and **verified on arrival**, so an author line is a fact rather than a claim.
Each post can be signed by one of your accounts, by an account under an alias,
or anonymously by a key made for that post and wiped — and accounts can rotate
their key. Posts written offline are kept and sent when the network returns,
and a node that was away recovers what it missed from its peers.

It is installable from a module catalog, runs in **Logos Basecamp 0.3.0**, and
was tested end to end between two real nodes on the public logos.test network,
driven through its own interface.

## Approach

**Two layers.** `core/` is a plain C++20 library that owns every rule of the
forum — what a post is, how it is signed and checked, what is stored, what is
sent and when, what is retried — and talks to the network only through a
three-method `Transport` interface. The Basecamp module (`src/`) wraps it: a
backend that connects the core to `delivery_module` and `storage_module`, and a
QML view. Keeping the rules out of the module is what let every reliability
behaviour (offline outbox, back-off, pacing, de-duplication, history) be tested
without a node, and it makes the core reusable by any other Logos app.

**Posts are content-addressed and self-authenticating.** A post's id is the
SHA-256 of its length-prefixed signing bytes; its signature covers every field.
A post edited in transit, or claiming someone else's key, is dropped before it
is stored; the same post arriving live, from a store node and from a snapshot
is stored once. *Rejected alternative:* the reference sample signs with
`keystore_signer` (secp256k1), which exposes no verify call, so its author
lines are claims. We keep Ed25519 keys (libsodium) in the forum's own store:
every reader can verify every post, and an anonymous post can have a key that
exists for one signature and is then zeroed.

**Identity and privacy.** *Identity* posts link to each other by design;
*alias* shows a chosen name but is still signed by the account (and says so);
*anonymous* uses a one-time key, so two anonymous posts cannot be linked to
each other or to the author. The privacy cost of one long-lived identity is
answered by rotation: an account can replace its key now, or automatically
after N posts or D days, and nothing links the old key to the new one.

**History on a network that keeps nothing.** We first used Store queries, as
the reference does. Measured on logos.test, all four fleet store nodes answer
`200 OK` with **no messages** — for the forum's topic, for its whole shard,
and with no filter at all: the fleet keeps no archive. A forum that relied on
it could never show a newcomer anything. The forum still queries store nodes
(a network that archives will work unchanged), and it also asks its peers: a
node coming online sends `{"logos-forum-want":1,"have":N}`; a peer holding
more posts saves a snapshot to **Logos Storage** and announces its CID with its
storage node's peer id and addresses; the newcomer dials that node directly
(two nodes behind NAT do not find each other's content through the DHT alone),
downloads the snapshot and merges it **post by post, each signature checked** —
a snapshot carries no authority. Snapshots are content-addressed, so peers
holding the same posts announce the same CID and it is fetched once; a peer
answers at most every 30 s.

**Reliability.** A post is written to SQLite and to an outbox before `Post`
returns, and leaves the outbox only when the network confirms it
(`messagePropagated` / `messageSent`). Tested with Basecamp cut off from the
network: `delivery_module` accepts the send and retries it only in memory
("No peers"), so treating acceptance as delivery — our first version — would
lose a post written offline if Basecamp were closed before the network came
back. Unconfirmed posts are sent again as soon as the connection returns, after two minutes otherwise, and after a restart;
duplicates are harmless because ids are content. The outbox is sent within a token bucket (5 at once, 20 per minute),
so a burst is paced, never dropped; a failed send — refused at once, or
accepted and later reported lost by `messageError` — is rescheduled with
exponential back-off (2 s … 5 min) and survives a restart.

**What running in the real host taught us** (each fixed and covered):
a synchronous `storeQuery` blocked the backend, so every module call that can
take time is asynchronous; a store node's last page carries
`"paginationCursor": null`, on which `json::value()` throws and the exception
aborted the ui-host, so every read of network or module JSON is typed and
non-throwing (the core has a test feeding it mistyped traffic); a synchronous
storage call made during storage_module's own event dispatch waited out the
20 s RPC timeout. And from the catalog: unpinned, Basecamp installs
`storage_module` 3.0.0-rc1, whose `uploadInit`/`downloadChunks` take different
arguments than the 2.1 series; the package manifest pins `~2.1.2`. No
`QRegularExpression` is used anywhere (under Basecamp's hardened runtime it
JIT-compiles and traps).

**Why the Logos stack.** A forum for speech people fear being punished for
cannot have an operator who can be compelled to censor, log or unmask. Logos
Delivery gives publication with no server to seize; Logos Storage gives
history no one hosts alone; signatures checked by every reader mean no one —
including us — can forge or silently edit a post. A centralised forum loses
all three: one takedown ends it, one subpoena unmasks it.

## FURPS Self-Assessment

### Functionality

Topics and replies; several accounts; three signing modes (account, alias,
anonymous one-time key); key rotation on demand or by policy; signature and
structure checks on every post (title ≤ 200 B, body ≤ 20 kB, alias ≤ 40 B);
content-addressed de-duplication; offline outbox; history from store nodes and
from peers' snapshots on Logos Storage. One forum ("Logos Forum") per install;
the content topic is derived from the forum name, so more can be added.
*Limits:* no editing or deletion (a signed post is immutable by construction);
no moderation beyond signatures — anyone can post; a returning node recovers
history only while some peer that has it is online, since logos.test store
nodes keep nothing, and only if the two storage nodes can reach each other
(observed: a node inside Docker's NAT received announcements but could not
fetch them); network-level anonymity is that of Logos Delivery (the
forum adds none of its own).

### Usability

Install from the catalog in three clicks; an account exists on first launch,
so the first post needs no setup. Topics are listed by latest activity with
author and reply count; each post shows how it was signed and the key it was
verified against. Pending and failure states are visible on the post itself and
in the header. [User guide](https://github.com/edenbd1/lp-0026-forum#using-the-forum).

### Reliability

Written to disk before it is sent; outbox retried with exponential back-off
(2 s … 5 min) across restarts; sends accepted and later lost are re-queued;
duplicates impossible by construction; malformed, forged or mistyped traffic is
dropped without exceptions (tested). History survives a fresh install through
peers' snapshots. All module calls that can block are asynchronous.

### Performance

A post is ~0.4 kB on the wire; delivery between two nodes on logos.test took
about 1 s. Sends are paced by a token bucket (5 burst, 20/min). A snapshot of
two posts is 840 bytes and was dialled, downloaded and merged within 4 s of its
announcement. Local reads are single SQLite queries with indexes on topic and
forum.

### Supportability

25 core tests (`core/tests`), each named for what it guarantees; an end-to-end
script that drives two real Basecamp nodes through the UI and checks each
node's store ([`scripts/e2e-two-nodes.sh`](https://github.com/edenbd1/lp-0026-forum/blob/main/scripts/e2e-two-nodes.sh));
CI on Linux and macOS, producing the merged two-platform package; reproducible package (a clean clone builds a
byte-identical `.lgx`); a per-install log at
`<user dir>/module_data/logos_forum/forum.log` (Basecamp does not surface a
ui-host's stderr); catalog generation and dependency pinning scripted.
